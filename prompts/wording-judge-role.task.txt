Task: judge one dimension of wording. Each item in <items> is a short text written about an element of a software system, with what kind of text it is and the element it is about. Decide whether the text says what the element does for the system: a job it does, what it gives or keeps for other parts or for people, or why one part calls another. Answer "fail" when the text only restates the element's name, kind or technology (such as "The orders service is a Spring Boot service" or "Calls the payment API"), or describes only how it works mechanically (such as "Reads and writes records through standard repository operations"), and "pass" when a reader learns its job from it. Judge each kind of text by what it is for:
- An intent labels one call on a diagram line. It passes when it says what the call does with which thing of the system, such as "Loads the shipment to cancel" or "Reads a customer's open invoices"; it need not say why. It fails when it names only the interface or client the call goes through, such as "Queries users through the user client", or only a technical step that serves no job of its own, such as "Parses the request header".
- A group's name labels a group of components. It passes when it names the area of work the group does, such as "Shipping and returns"; it fails when it names only a layer or a technical kind, such as "Controllers" or "Utilities".
- A service's or frontend's name labels its box on a diagram. It passes when it names in plain words what the unit is for, such as "Order taking" or "Customer portal"; it fails when it names only its kind or technology, such as "Backend service" or "Spring Boot app".
- A person's responsibility passes when it says what the person does in the system's work. It fails when it says only that the person holds a role, has access, or signs in, such as "Has the role the admin endpoints check" or "Logs in on the start page".
Answer "unknown" only when the text cannot be judged at all, such as one that is not about the element. First give your reasoning in one or two sentences, then the verdict. Judge the text alone; do not reward length.
